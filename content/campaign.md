# Staalfront Campaigns

Two original faction campaigns, ten missions each. The campaigns share the same conflict but reveal different evidence and motives. The broad inspiration is classic 1990s real-time strategy: readable objectives, varied mission types, escalating stakes, and briefings that establish a tactical problem. All characters, dialogue, locations, and plot events here are original.

## Faction 1: The Meridian Compact

A coalition of border states trying to keep transport corridors and civilian infrastructure functioning after coordinated attacks. Its strengths are flexible logistics and dependable field engineering; its weakness is political fragmentation.

### Story interludes
- **After F1-M03 — The Missing Manifest:** recovered freight records show that fuel and radios were redirected before the first attack, suggesting advance knowledge.
- **After F1-M06 — Waterline:** engineers confirm that the dam damage was deliberate, but the charge design does not match either faction's equipment.
- **Before F1-M10 — The Archive Key:** the relay logs identify a private logistics-and-intelligence network that has been sending forged orders to both sides.

### Story interludes
- **After F2-M03 — The Passenger List:** evacuees describe identical orders arriving in villages on both sides, each designed to provoke retaliation.
- **After F2-M08 — The Ledger Copies:** the convoy's records connect shell companies to the same logistics broker.
- **Before F2-M10 — No Flag in the Signal:** both campaigns independently identify the same abandoned coordination hub.

## Campaign checks
- Each faction has ten missions and at least one commando, escort, or infiltration mission without conventional base-building.
- No campaign is built entirely from base destruction; mission types alternate between conquest, escort, infiltration, defence, deception, sabotage, and time pressure.
- Difficulty rises overall, with a lighter mission after major set pieces where possible.
- New unit roles are introduced by a mission that explicitly teaches their use.
- The two campaigns overlap in world events but have different objectives and reveal different pieces of the mystery.
- **Finale chronology and knowledge:** F1-M10 secures the regional military archive before the final vault decision in F2-M10. Elian circulates authenticated extracts to military commands on both sides, not the complete hub archive to the public. Receipt does not imply that every field unit has read, accepted or acted on them. F2-M10 takes place at a separate abandoned network hub and recovers the original transmission keys, templates, payments and any collected correspondence. Sava's choice concerns public disclosure of this fuller evidence, not whether to send the first warning. The campaigns can be played in either order; this chronology does not require cross-campaign completion flags to unlock missions.
